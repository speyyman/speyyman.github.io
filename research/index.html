Research

Forecasting and modelling with real-world data, which is usually messier than it looks. I try to report where a result breaks as clearly as where it holds.

Before modelling, I clean the data and record every value I had to fill in, so I can say how much of a result rests on estimates. I start with a simple baseline, so every model has to beat something reasonable.

I test models on stretches of time they have never seen. It is stricter than testing on shuffled data, and it gives a more realistic picture of how a forecast will do in use.

Questions I keep coming back to: how much of a forecast is skill, and how much is luck? When two sensors disagree, which one should you believe? And what does a model look like when it is honest about what it does not know?
