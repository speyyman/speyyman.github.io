Case study · Tool

Plate

The dither engine behind this site: procedural engravings, rendered live in your browser.

Year
2026
Role
Design and build
Status
Live on this site, and open source
Built with
TypeScript, Canvas, WebGL, React

Overview

Plate turns a few lines of maths into an engraving. Every image on this site, from the sunflower to the moon, is drawn in your browser and pushed through the same three-tone dither.

There are no image files to download. A fern is four rules, a sunflower is a single angle repeated, and the browser does the rest.

The knot

I wanted a site that looked printed rather than templated, without shipping heavy images or leaning on a stock look. It also had to stay quick on a phone, which rules out most of the easy ways of making a page feel rich.

My role

I designed and built the whole engine: the generators, the dither, the palette and the way plates load, as well as the site that uses them.

The art direction was part of the engineering. Choosing three tones and sticking to them did more for the look than any single drawing.

How it went

Each plate is a small generator. One places seeds at the golden angle, another iterates a fern from four rules, another grows a tree, and another draws the moon in tonight’s phase.

Every drawing is then reduced to the same three tones. That shared palette is what lets unrelated subjects sit side by side and still read as one set.

The drawing happens quietly. Plates are built one at a time, and the dithering runs on the graphics card, so the page never stalls while it loads.

Where it landed

The whole site stays small, because the art is code rather than pictures.

Adding a new plate takes an afternoon: write the generator, give it a caption, and it joins the set.

The engine is now a library of its own, plate-engine, published on npm with no dependencies: npm install plate-engine. Every generator shares one interface, with parameters a slider can drive, and its tests check that it still draws this site’s plates bit for bit.

Plate Studio puts it in a browser tab: pick a generator or drop in a picture, choose a dither and a palette, and download a PNG or BMP, including at the exact size of an e-reader’s screen. Your picture never leaves your device.

Decisions I’d defend

Drawing everything in code instead of using images meant the site stayed tiny and the look stayed consistent. It also gave the site its theme, because I could only draw what has a rule behind it.

Running the dither on the graphics card kept scrolling smooth. The same work on the main thread would have made the page stutter.
