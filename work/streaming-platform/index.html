Case study · Product

Streaming platform

Music streaming with two audio qualities, offline playback and a self-serve artist portal.

Year
2025 – 2026
Role
Architecture, backend and web app
Status
Delivered
Built with
Microservices, CDN delivery, token-signed downloads, web audio

This is a music streaming platform built for listeners who often have a weak internet connection. Every track is stored in two qualities, a light 128 kbps stream and a fuller 320 kbps one, so the music keeps playing where bandwidth is scarce.

Most streaming services assume a fast, steady connection. This one also needed offline listening, and a way for artists to upload and manage their own music. Each of those touches storage, delivery and access control at the same time, so a change in one place tends to affect the others.

I split the system into small services that each own one job, such as uploads, encoding, the catalogue, playback and downloads, and put a CDN, a network of servers close to the listeners, in front of the audio. Every upload is encoded at both bitrates. The player starts on the light stream and moves up when the connection allows, instead of asking the listener to choose.

Offline playback runs through a download manager that issues tokens valid for 24 hours, so music plays without a signal but a download cannot be copied and kept forever. The artist portal handles uploads, track details and listening statistics, so artists can publish a release without asking anyone to process it.

I designed the architecture and built the services, the download manager and the portal, working independently from the first sketch to the handover, which included documentation and a walkthrough. If I did it again, I would design the analytics alongside playback from the start rather than adding them last.
