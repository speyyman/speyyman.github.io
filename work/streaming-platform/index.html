Case study · Product

Streaming platform

Music streaming with dual-quality audio, offline playback and a self-serve artist portal.

Year
2025 – 2026
Role
Architecture, backend and web app
Status
Delivered
Built with
Microservices, CDN delivery, token-signed downloads, web audio

Overview

A cultural music streaming platform built for people who do not always have a good connection. Every track is available in two qualities, a lean 128 kbps stream and a fuller 320 kbps one, so the music keeps playing even where bandwidth is scarce.

Around the player sit two things that make it a real service: an offline download manager, and a self-service portal where artists upload their own music, manage its details and watch how it is received.

The knot

Most streaming services are designed for fast, steady connections, and quietly fail everywhere else. This one had to work for listeners with very little bandwidth, and still carry the weight of offline playback, uploads and analytics.

Each of those touches storage, delivery and access control at once, so a change in one place tends to ripple through the others.

My role

I was the engineer on this one. I designed the architecture, built the services behind the player, and wrote the offline download manager and the artist portal.

Working independently meant the whole path, from a file uploaded by an artist to a track playing on a slow connection, was mine to keep coherent.

How it went

I split the system into small services that each own one job, such as uploads, encoding, catalogue, playback and downloads, and put a CDN in front of the audio so tracks are served from somewhere close to the listener.

Every upload is encoded at both bitrates and stored under one track. The player starts on the lean stream and moves up only when the connection allows, instead of asking the listener to choose and hoping for the best.

Offline playback runs through a download manager that hands out tokens valid for 24 hours. Music plays without a signal, but a download cannot be copied and kept forever. The artist portal gives artists uploads, metadata and analytics in one place, with no support ticket needed to publish.

Where it landed

Listeners on weak connections get music that starts quickly and keeps going, and those with a better signal get the fuller sound.

Artists publish on their own schedule, and nobody on the team has to hand-process a release.

Decisions I’d defend

Choosing a low bitrate as the starting point, not the fallback, was the decision that fitted the audience. The first thing a listener hears should arrive fast everywhere, and quality can improve from there.

Splitting the platform into small services meant a problem in one part, like a slow upload, did not take playback down with it. It also made each piece easy to explain to whoever inherits it.
